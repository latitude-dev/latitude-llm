---
title: How to use Latitude
description: The recommended workflow for finding, tracking, and fixing production issues in your AI agent.
---

Latitude works best as a continuous loop for production agents: observe real traffic, find failure modes, turn them into trackable issues, watch them, fix them, and keep checking for regressions.

## The core workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect telemetry">
    Send traces from your agent to Latitude. Each user interaction becomes a trace with the LLM calls, tool calls, metadata, user id, and session id needed to understand what happened.

    If you have not connected your app yet, follow [Start tracing](../telemetry/start-tracing).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Explore traffic with search">
    Open the **Traces** page and use the search bar to find conversations that match behaviours you care about: frustrated users, failed payments, hallucinated answers, tool loops, policy problems, or any domain-specific issue.

    Semantic search ranks the most relevant traces for exploration. When a cohort is worth revisiting, capture it with filters or exact text and save it as a [saved search](../search/saved-searches) your team can review over time. Semantic queries themselves are for exploring and are not saved.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Annotate what is good or bad">
    Open traces from the search results or the trace list and leave [annotations](../annotations/overview). A thumbs-down annotation with clear feedback tells Latitude this behaviour is worth tracking.

    Latitude's [flaggers](../annotations/flaggers) also annotate common failure categories automatically, such as frustration, refusal, jailbreaking, tool errors, and empty responses.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let Latitude cluster issues">
    Failed annotations, flagger matches, evaluation failures, and custom scores become [scores](../scores/overview). Latitude groups similar failed scores into [issues](../issues/overview): recurring failure patterns with names, descriptions, examples, trends, and lifecycle states.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Triage and monitor">
    Triage issues on the Issues page: inspect example traces, set priority, and resolve noise. Latitude watches the issue lifecycle for you with three built-in [monitors](../monitors/overview) that notify your team when an issue is discovered, when it regresses, and when it escalates. Notifications can go to in-app, email, or Slack.

    You can also generate [evaluations](../evaluations/overview) that score the behaviour on live traffic, and point your own monitors at a saved search to be alerted when matching traces start arriving or spike.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fix your agent and repeat">
    Fix the underlying behaviour in your code, prompts, tools, retrieval, or product flow. Resolve the issue once it is fixed, and the regressed monitor tells you if it comes back. Then repeat the loop as new production traffic arrives.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What to focus on first

If you are setting up Latitude for the first time:

1. Connect tracing for one production agent.
2. Add `userId` and `sessionId` so traces can be grouped by user and conversation.
3. On the Traces page, search for one failure mode your team already cares about.
4. Annotate representative traces with specific feedback.
5. Watch the Issues page for clustered patterns, and let the built-in monitors notify you.
6. Generate evaluations for the issues you want to score continuously.

## Why this works

Latitude does not require you to define every possible failure upfront. You discover issues from real traffic, validate them with human review, and turn important patterns into automated monitoring. Over time, the system becomes a living map of what goes wrong in your agent and whether your fixes are working.
